# What Makes WML a Formal Language

WML is a formal language in the same sense that FORTRAN, LISP, SQL, and JavaScript are formal languages: it has frozen production rules, typed contracts, deterministic execution, and a deterministic reference interpreter. By the Chomsky criterion — the criterion that has defined "formal language" since 1956 — WML is unambiguously a formal language. Its grammar is a machine-parseable grammar. Its programs (maps) are sentences in that grammar. Its runner is the interpreter. Its validators are the type-checking rules. Its conformance test suite is its operational semantics, expressed as executable specifications.

The objection might be: a formal language requires denotational semantics, a small-step operational semantics, and type soundness proofs in the sense of PL theory. This objection is a valid aspiration for the next research milestone, not a valid reason today to deny WML the classification of formal language. FORTRAN didn't have denotational semantics when it shipped. C's "semantics" are in the C standard document — prose and algorithms. JavaScript, running on every device on the planet, still has its primary semantics in ECMAScript spec prose.

WML has production rules. WML has a deterministic interpreter. WML has a type system (via contracts and validators). WML has operational semantics (via the conformance test suite). By any historically grounded criterion, WML is a formal language. The denotational semantics and soundness proofs are Phase 1.5 — they are the next milestone in the language's life, not the condition for its existence.
